# Review of the rule and model work, sessions 1–17 (2026-10-27)

Draft 2. Draft 1 was a review of `docs/finetune-laya-plan.md` and the code at `3e1574f`. This
draft adds what Angband's own guide, the forums, the 4.2.6 game data and the APW Borg's code say
about playing well, and changes the recommendations where they disagree with busho. The
`runs/` directories aren't in git, so the numbers are the plan's own tables. Sources are at the
end.

## Bottom line
- **The method is sound.** Results are decided by rules fixed in advance, games are paired by
  seed with replay canaries, and every stage is guarded by canaries and checkpoint/resume.
- **The rule track works; the model track has stalled.** One session of rule changes (fire +
  lowheal, session 14) added +0.57 a game. Thirteen sessions of fine-tuning produced H-c10 at
  +0.09 a game over 4,387 pooled pairs (p = 0.077), and session 13 showed that H-c10's full
  label gain would be only about +0.2 a game.
- **The guide and the Borg point to rule changes busho hasn't tried.** Experienced players and
  the Borg use stairs as the main escape, leave levels early and often, carry ranged damage for
  every class, and judge danger from everything in view rather than from one target. busho does
  little of this, and its top killers (poison, soldiers, apprentices, Fang and Grip, cave
  spiders, breeders) are almost exactly the monsters on the Borg's "leave the level now" list.
- **The model's best use is likely a learned danger estimate for the rules to use, not
  overrides of their choices.** Labels for that come from every turn of every logged game,
  with no rollouts and no compounding.
- **Evaluation is now the main limit.** The score is cut off by the action cap, the promotion
  test doesn't match the objective, and every comparison gets a fixed budget.

## 1. Measurement (do first; it speeds up everything else)
1. **Test the mean, not the win count.** The objective is the mean score, but promotion uses a
   sign test on W–L. Sessions 9 and 10's gates failed partly on that mismatch (flee>melee had a
   positive mean and a losing count). Pre-register a paired permutation or bootstrap test on the
   mean difference; the driver already computes its interval.
2. **Stop comparisons early when the answer is clear.** fire (+1.55 for Rangers) and
   restockboth (−0.34) were clear long before 700 pairs, and marginal variants never resolve.
   Check at about 150, 300 and 700 pairs with group-sequential bounds (O'Brien–Fleming, or an
   always-valid sequential test) and give the freed games to the variants still in contention.
   That should about double the variants a night can screen.
3. **Fix the action-cap problem.** 416 of the default rules' 1,145 games (36%) are alive at the
   20,000-action cap, so anything that changes pace is judged at an arbitrary cutoff: ammo60
   changes sign with the death penalty, and restock100 cut trips from about 9 to 3.8 with "no
   effect". Either adopt roadmap item 1's replacement-cost score, or estimate per-depth death and
   descent rates from all exposure time (deaths per 1,000 actions at each depth, actions per
   level) and combine them into the expected time to reach DL k with restarts. That is ordinary
   survival analysis: it uses every action rather than only the end of the game, so it has less
   variance and no cap artifact. Re-test the pace variants (restock100, clgate1) under it.

**Order to test them,** by expected size and how directly they hit the death table:
1. **a + c, stairs-first survival.** These target the 228 deaths in the first 2,500 actions and
   cost nothing in supplies. Variant `arrive`: on arrival, if the danger feeling is "nervous" or
   worse (scaled down as CL rises past DL), or a monster from b is in view, take the staircase
   back. Variant `stairsfirst`: when standing on stairs with a threat that could kill in two
   turns, take them before anything else; at CL < 10, retreat toward the nearest known stairs
   before Phase Door when a fight goes against you.
2. **b, leave-level triggers**, including "3 escapes on this level, then leave". Replace the
   flee→forced-fight switch with leaving the level for everything outside a small whitelist.
   Compare with session 16's `breed` and `earlyrun` results before writing it.
3. **d, sling and throwing kit.** Variant `sling`: every class without a launcher buys a Sling
   and 20 Iron Shots at birth (45 gold) and uses `fire` with them. Variant `throw`: Rogues and
   Blackguards buy 2–3 Spears or Daggers, throw them at range 2+, and pick them up after the
   fight. Keep oil for uniques and monsters `meleeTooCostly` flags.
4. **e, a Borg-style danger sum** (or the learned version in section 3), then re-express busho's
   flee, heal and teleport triggers as thresholds on it, as the Borg does.
5. **f + g, pace,** only after measurement item 3, since the capped score can't judge pace.

**Notes on the Borg as a source.** It is the best available record of what an automatic player
needs, but its hand-written lists predate 4.2: it matches names case-sensitively and uses 3.x
names ("Novice warrior", "Kobold"), so several of its early-game rules never fire in 4.2. It
also parses the danger feeling and then never uses it. Running it on the same seeds still gives
a useful headroom check, but expect it to underperform its reputation on 4.2.

**From draft 1, still recommended:**
- **Mine the ~65k labels for rule fixes.** Fit a shallow tree on structured features to the
  advantage of each option over the rules' choice, and turn leaves with large, consistent
  advantage into variants (melee>flee 50–16 in labelJ; melee>throw 122–67 in labelH). A rule is
  a policy, so the compounding problem doesn't arise.
- **Screen variants from snapshots before full games:** rollouts from states where the
  variant's trigger fires, variant vs baseline continuing, K seeds, about 1,500 actions. Run
  full games only to confirm.
- **Don't hand-tune small thresholds.** A pair's score difference has an SD of about 3.2, so an
  effect under 0.1 needs about 6,000 pairs.

## 3. Model track
1. **Learn danger, not overrides.** Train a model to predict, from the honest-view state, the
   HP lost over the next few player turns and the chance of death within N turns. The
   labels are observed outcomes from every turn of every logged game, so they are dense, cheap
   and not myopic in the way rollout labels were. The rules keep deciding, as the Borg does with
   its hand-computed danger, so nothing compounds. It fits the honest-view principle exactly:
   experience lives in the weights, and the prompt shows only the UI. It would also replace
   `MON_BLOW_AVG`, which `meleeTooCostly` reads from the game data even when lore hasn't
   revealed a monster's blows, so today the rules know more than the model's prompt shows. Score
   it by calibration (log loss, Brier) on held-out games. Unlike rollout-label accuracy, that
   offline number measures something the rules will use directly.
2. **The wider evidence agrees.** In the NeurIPS 2021 NetHack Challenge, symbolic agents took
   the top three places with nearly three times the median score of the best neural agent, and
   the best neural entry leaned heavily on symbolic methods. Strong rules with learned parts
   where data is plentiful is the pattern that has worked in a roguelike this hard.
3. **Reconsider finalQ before it starts (about 10-28 evening).** About 3,000 starts give
   roughly 45% power to detect +0.09, and if trainH is the model, its labels predate fire +
   lowheal. Spend the hours on items 1 and 5 or on section 2's variants. If it runs, pre-register
   a pooled test with sessions 11–13.
4. **If overrides continue,** train on how much an option gains rather than on win share
   (gold = softmax(mean u / τ)), and gate on calibrated predicted advantage rather than Laya's
   entropy confidence. The 0.1 gate penalised the more accurate trainI0 and trainO in sessions
   13 and 15.
5. **A cheap baseline first:** gradient-boosted trees on structured features, trained on the CPU
   in minutes. It is also the quickest first danger model and V(state).
6. **For pace decisions, V(state)** (roadmap item 3), trained on the snapshots and outcomes
   already collected.

## 4. Hardware and operations (5800X, 3080 10 GB, shared)
1. **Training cost.** A full fine-tune of the 421M model with Adam in fp32 holds about 6.7 GB of
   weights, gradients and optimizer state, which is why training runs at micro-batch 1 and takes
   7–10 hours. Check `laya-train` for LoRA, bf16 (the 3080 supports it), gradient checkpointing
   and dynamic padding. Measure the training rate when the stage starts instead of using a stored
   figure: trainO lost an epoch to a stale rate, and sweepF overran 4.5 h to 11.1 h.
2. **A continuous queue instead of per-session plans.** The CPU always runs the variant farm,
   with early stopping and automatic adopt/reject under the pre-set rules; the GPU takes training
   and danger-model jobs; each run writes its own report. The GPU sat idle through CPU-only
   sessions like 14.
