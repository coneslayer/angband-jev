# Headroom first: plan from 2026-11-09

Status: proposed 2026-11-09, nothing built or launched. It follows the big-picture look of the same day and replaces
"Next phase: deeper play across all classes" in `docs/finetune-laya-plan.md` as the working plan. The record up to
2026-11-01 stays in that file and in `docs/report-2026-10-30.md`.

## Summary

**The project's question** (user, 2026-11-09): this is curiosity-driven work, and the aim is to learn what value a
decision model brings to an automated player. A stronger player is the instrument, not the goal. So every phase
below is judged by what it tells us about the model, and a well-measured "none here" counts as a result.

Progress has stalled because the search method has run out, not the ideas. Rule screens found and repaired
malfunctions; those are mostly used up, and what is left is too small for a one-rule screen to see. The player is
still weak in absolute terms, so the headroom is large, but nobody has measured where it is. The model was tested in
one role, at six decision points that turned out to have almost nothing to give to any chooser. This plan first
checks whether the model can deliver a gain that is known to exist (phase M1), measures where the headroom is
(phases 0 to 2), changes how rules are screened so that the games also become training data (phase 3), and then
puts the model on the decisions that carry leverage (phases M2 and M3).

| Phase | Question it answers | Build | Farm time |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1. Rule knockouts | Take a hand rule with a known gain out of the base: does a decision model win the gain back? | about 1.5 days | one night for the first rule, about three more for the other two |
| 0. Scoreboard | How far does a fresh character get, per life? | half a day | about 3 hours |
| 1. Headroom map | Which kind of advantage, if the player had it, would cut deaths by a third or more? | about 1 day | one night |
| 2. Borg reference | Does a rule player exist on this engine that gets much deeper per life, as a Warrior and as each pure caster? | half a day to 1 day | a pilot, then one to two nights (the Borg's speed isn't known) |
| 3. Factorial screens | Which of many small rules and settings help, tested all at once? | about 1.5 days | every night after |
| M2. Decision headroom | Which new decisions would repay a perfect chooser? | half a day each | hours each |
| M3. The ladder | On those decisions, what does each kind of decider capture: a fixed setting, a hand rule, trees, the decision model, hosted Jev? | per decision | per decision |

M1 and phases 0 to 2 are independent and can be built in any order; M1 is first because it uses what exists and its
answer changes how everything after it is read. Phase 3's factor list waits for phases 1 and 2; M2 and M3 wait for
phase 1 and use phase 3's games.

## Why: what the record shows

Sources: the report, the plan's 10-30 to 11-01 sections, and two analyses of local run data made on 2026-11-09
(`runs/harvest/2026-10-30-full/harvest.jsonl`, 3,692 fresh games; `runs/deep/2026-10-31/deepS/base`, 1,159 games).

1. **The wins were repairs.** The base holds 18 changes. Most stop a malfunction or a stall: never buying upgrades
   (gearid), recall then stairs (worwait), tunnelling granite (digskill), the full-pack walk (invfix), dark stalls
   (lightout), spells never cast (spellfx), resting while blind (blindsafe). More than 50 arms were rejected, and
   most of those added a tactic: corridors, devices, gear sets, confusion escapes, escape restocking, lookahead.

2. **The absolute level is low.** Fresh games of the full harvest (60,000-action cap, base before blindsafe):

   | Class | Fresh games | Reach DL5 | DL10 | DL15 | DL20 | Death per level, DL1–9 | DL10–14 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Warrior | 67 | 94% | 66% | 24% | 3.0% | 5% | 18% |
   | Blackguard | 310 | 83% | 40% | 4.5% | 0.3% | 10% | 35% |
   | Paladin | 154 | 77% | 38% | 13% | 1.9% | 10% | 20% |
   | Rogue | 212 | 82% | 42% | 9.0% | 2.4% | 9% | 26% |
   | Ranger | 237 | 79% | 36% | 5.9% | 0.4% | 11% | 30% |
   | Priest | 449 | 69% | 30% | 3.8% | 0.2% | 13% | 34% |
   | Druid | 588 | 43% | 16% | 2.6% | 0% | 19% | 30% |
   | Mage | 1,675 | 42% | 10% | 0.7% | 0% | 22% | 42% |

   Death per level is 1 − (share reaching the deeper level / share reaching the shallower one)^(1/levels). On deep
   starts, 151 of 153 base games died within 20,000 actions, about 2.2 levels on. A game that is to go far needs a
   death rate per level one or two orders of magnitude lower, which no sum of 5% gains reaches.

3. **The remaining rule ideas are below the screens' resolution.** At 700 pairs the sd of log PH is about 0.12 on
   deep starts (blindsafe: PH 0.68, z 3.20) and about 0.04 on fresh games (deepkit: PH 0.865, z 3.53), so a screen
   resolves roughly PH 0.82 on deep starts and 0.93 on fresh ones. Each deep-start rule touched a few percent of the
   deaths. Two screen winners (teleshop+confesc, escdire) failed their confirmations.

4. **Switching options at decision points gains almost nothing.** In the 10-31 checks the option explained 11% of
   the outcome variance at dangerous fight and tactic decisions and 9% at strategic ones; death within 2,000 actions
   was about 62% whichever fight option was taken. That closes the model's present role, choosing among the rules'
   options with the rules carrying on. It doesn't show that the play is near its best: the rollouts kept the same
   executor and the same character.

5. **Character strength has only been seen in a narrow band.** In the deep base arm the median start is DL11, CL12,
   about 100 HP, AC 33. Levels gained by the CL − DL margin at the start:

   | CL − DL | Starts | Levels gained | Share gaining 3+ |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | −3 or less | 27 | 0.63 | 11% |
   | −2 to −1 | 159 | 1.24 | 13% |
   | 0 to 1 | 569 | 1.90 | 28% |
   | 2 to 3 | 360 | 2.19 | 32% |
   | 4 to 5 | 35 | 2.31 | 40% |
   | 6 or more | 9 | about 5 | 8 of 9 |

   The trend holds within Paladins, Rangers, Rogues and Blackguards, not within Warriors, Mages or Priests. Only 9 of
   1,159 starts have a margin of 6 or more, because the descent rule never produces them (from DL10 it asks for CL
   ≥ 0.7 × DL, and a stay of twice the too-long threshold waives even that). The bank can't say what a strong
   character would do at depth.

6. **Two explanations checked and dropped.**
   - *The pace-weighted score rejects good rules.* For every rejected deep-start arm, deaths per level gained
     (death ratio / pace ratio) was 0.94 or worse, so a score without the pace term would have rejected them too.
   - *The characters are simply under-levelled against the Borg's bar.* At DL10–19 `borg-prepared.c` asks CL ≥ DL
     for most classes (Warrior and Blackguard DL − 4, Mage DL + 5), which busho about meets. The Borg also asks for
     2 teleports, 3 Cure Critical Wounds and See Invisible or telepathy at DL10, Free Action at DL20, and CL ≥ DL +
     5 to 13 from DL21. Whether those matter here is what phases 1 and 2 measure.

## Principles kept

- **The player and its prompts see only what the UI shows.** Phase 1's cheats are measurements, like rollout labels
  that read the true state. A cheat never enters the base, cheat games are logged under their own arm names, and
  their danger logs are never pooled or used for training.
- **Decision rules are written down before a run starts**, in this file, with the launch line.
- **Nothing is adopted without a confirmation on fresh seeds** (the 11-01 lesson).
- **PH stays the verdict score for adoption**: PH(10) on fresh 20,000-action games, PH(20) from DL10 on bank starts
  with lineage clusters. Phase 0 adds whole-life numbers beside it.
- **Housekeeping as before**: disk guards on `/` and `C:`, one tar per finished run to `/mnt/x/angband-jev`, a
  replay canary before every run on a new engine or busho build.

## What is known so far about the model's value

| Role tested | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Local models zero-shot (Kev-0.8B, Laya) | at chance: 38–50% on 16 clear-cut cases, a constant answer 44% | report, model track |
| Hosted Jev zero-shot, on rollout labels | 47% agreement against the rules' 40% and fine-tuned Laya's 61% (393 records, v1 labels) | plan, Jev probe 10-14 |
| Laya fine-tuned on rollout labels, overriding the rules at six questions | about 60% against 40% on held-out labels; in play 51.2% of decided pairs (p 0.077), never promoted | sessions 6–16 |
| Why play didn't follow | overrides close together don't add up: about 95% of the predicted gain was lost | session 13 |
| A perfect chooser at those questions (long rollouts, held-out pick) | +0.085 ± 0.046 levels per dangerous decision, +0.073 ± 0.060 per strategic one | 10-31 checks |
| A learned danger estimate (trees) feeding the rules | predicts well (death in 20 turns: AUC 0.926 against 0.792 by hand); both uses in play rejected | sessions 17–20 |

Read together: the model learned what it was taught, and what it was taught was worth little, because the six
questions carry little. That says nothing yet about decisions that do carry leverage. Two things are untested: whether
the whole chain (labels, training, gating, play) can deliver a gain when one is known to be there, and whether the
headroom check itself would see such a gain. M1 tests both.

## Phase M1: rule knockouts

The hand rules are the best-measured gains in the project. Each one is also a decision: a class of states and a
choice. Taking a rule out of the base leaves a hole of known size, and the question is whether a decision model,
asked at those states, fills it. This is the positive control the model track never had.

### Rules

Three rules with a gain measured on their own, of three different kinds:

| Rule | Kind | Known gain | Decision put to the model | Options |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| blindsafe | tactical, in the moment | PH(20) 0.68 and 0.64, bank starts | blind, with a monster adjacent, hits taken while blind, or a monster seen within 7 in the last 100 game turns | rest; drink a cure; take the stairs if on them; walk away |
| tryunknown | knowledge of the game | PH(10) 0.67, fresh starts | an unknown potion in the pack and no monster in view | drink it now; keep it; leave it for the Alchemist |
| leave | strategic, avoidance | PH(10) 0.65, fresh starts | on arrival at a level, and after each escape, poisoning or bleeding | stay; take the nearest stairs |

The state class is wider than the rule's own trigger on purpose. If the question were asked only where the hand
rule fires, a constant "yes" would recover the gain and nothing would be learned. The prompt shows only what the UI
shows, as always.

### Arms, per rule, against the base with the rule knocked out

| Arm | What it shows |
|---|---|
| the hand rule | the size of the hole (repeats the known gain on these starts) |
| Jev 1.13 zero-shot | whether a pretrained decision model already knows what took log-reading to find |
| Laya zero-shot | the same for the local model |
| Laya fine-tuned on labels at this decision | whether our data teaches it, and how many labels that takes (trained on 1,000, 4,000 and all) |
| trees on the danger-log features, same labels | whether a decision model adds anything over a plain learner |
| the long-rollout pick itself, where affordable | the ceiling the labels allow |

Labels come from snapshots at the decision in knocked-out base games: force each option, continue with the
knocked-out base, u = levels gained − 3 if dead. Two sets a rule:
- **check set**: 400 decisions at the 10-31 long-horizon setting (8 paired seeds, 2,000 actions), about 3 farm hours;
- **training set**: 4,000 decisions at 4 seeds and 500 actions, about 5 farm hours. Whether the cheaper labels agree
  with the long ones is measured on the check set's decisions before anything is trained on them.

Every arm's answers are gated the same way and fall back to the knocked-out base.

### Steps

blindsafe goes first, alone: bank games are short, so its labels and its play arms fit in one night and a morning.
tryunknown and leave follow (about three more nights) unless blindsafe's reading rule says to stop and repair.

1. **Offline.** On the check set, for every arm: agreement with the rollout pick, regret, and calibration. Also what
   the 10-31 headroom measure says here: the held-out gain of the rollout pick, and the share of outcome variance the
   option explains. Jev calls for all three rules cost about $0.03.
2. **In play.** Each arm against the knocked-out base by the existing sequential screen on the rule's own kind of
   starts (blindsafe on the bank, the other two fresh). Jev arms run at 4 games at a time and stop at 300 pairs;
   every call goes through the spend ledger (cap $1 for this phase, expected about $0.40; fallbacks counted, the arm
   invalid above 2%).
3. **Score**: the share of the hand rule's gain recovered, log PH(arm) / log PH(hand rule), with its interval.

### Reading rules (fixed now)

- **The headroom check sees the hole** (held-out gain of the rollout pick at least three times the +0.085 found at
  the six questions): the 10-31 conclusion stands as measured. **It doesn't**: the check is too blunt to close a
  decision, and the six questions are reopened with a better one before M2 uses it as a gate.
- **Zero-shot Jev recovers half or more of a rule's gain**: a pretrained model can stand in for rule-writing. The
  next step is to ask it at states where no rule exists yet (M2's candidates), before any training.
- **Only fine-tuned Laya recovers it**: the model needs our labels; the label count that sufficed sets the budget
  for M3.
- **Trees match the decision model**: the value is in the labels, not in the model class. M3 then runs trees first
  and the decision model only where state text holds something the features don't.
- **No arm recovers half, though the offline numbers look good**: the fault is between labels and play (gating,
  compounding, the wider state class). Find it here, where the right answer is known, before any new decision.
- **No arm does well offline either**: the model can't read these states. Fix the state text and repeat step 1.

## Phase 1: headroom map

Give the player one large advantage at a time and see how far deaths fall. An advantage that barely helps marks an
axis not worth more rules; one that helps a lot marks where to work, and the next question is how to earn it honestly.

### Build

- **Engine** (`angband-c`, `main-http.c`): `POST /cheat {"grant":"a,b"}`, refused unless the server was started with
  `--cheats`. Lasting grants live in one `cheat` struct that `calc_bonuses` and the command loop consult. A `/fork`
  child inherits it; a savepoint restore starts a new process, so busho sends the grant again.
- **busho**: `CHEAT=a,b` in the environment sends the grant once, after birth or restore, before the first action.
  The danger-log header records it.
- **driver18 / driver17**: a variant spelled `cheat:a+b` sets `CHEAT` instead of adding to `RULEFIX`.

| Arm | Grant | How |
|---|---|---|
| `cl10` | +10 character levels | experience raised through `player_exp_gain`, so HP, skills and spells follow |
| `hp2` | doubled hit points | the character's HP-per-level table doubled |
| `speed10` | +10 speed | cheat term in `calc_bonuses` |
| `offense` | +1 blow and +10 to-dam | cheat term in `calc_bonuses` (melee and missiles; read by class) |
| `supplies` | Phase Door never below 10; Cure Light, Serious and Critical Wounds never below 5 each | topped up before each command; the kinds made known |
| `tele` | Teleportation scrolls never below 5 | as `supplies` |
| `protect` | protection from blindness and confusion | cheat flags in `calc_bonuses` |
| `esp` | telepathy | cheat flag in `calc_bonuses` |
| `know` | all runes and flavours known, full monster recall | `player_learn_all_runes`, the flavour routine behind `birth_know_flavors`, the wizard recall routine |
| `gold` | +50,000 gold | once |
| `all` | every grant above | |

### Checks before the run

- With no cheat sent, 12 base starts replay identically on the new engine and busho builds.
- Each grant on 8 starts: the effect shows in `/state` (CL, HP, speed, blows, counts, flags), no errors or loops.
- `cl10` makes the descent rule's `overleveled` test true, so the character dives faster. That is the rules'
  response to strength and is left in; the death-rate ratio below is stratified by depth, so it still reads the
  strength itself.

### Runs

| Run | Starts | Arms | Games | Time on 13 slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| deep | 150 bank starts, DL10–19, as the deep screens | base + 11 | 1,800 | about 1.5 hours |
| fresh | 150 fresh starts, class rotation, 20,000 actions | base + 11 | 1,800 | about 3.5 hours |

One look at 150 pairs, no sequential bounds: this is a measurement, not an adoption screen. Reported per arm, with 80%
intervals: the stage-stratified death-rate ratio (deaths per action at the same deepest level), the pace ratio,
deaths per level gained, PH, and the same by class.

### Reading rule (fixed now)

On the death-rate ratio against base, per run:
- **live**: 0.67 or lower;
- **dead**: 0.85 or higher;
- **between**: rerun that arm to 300 pairs the same night and read it again; still between counts as weak.

| Result | What follows |
|---|---|
| `cl10` or `hp2` live | strength against depth is the lever: the descent margin, farming and kill quotas go into phase 3 as settings, and "descend, farm or go up" is M2's first candidate |
| `offense` live | weapon and blows choice: find why gearset lost (PH 1.38 at 150 pairs) before anything else |
| `speed10` live | Potions of Speed, burden and speed items get value in shopping and gear choice |
| `supplies` or `tele` live, `gold` live | the gold economy: what is bought first, and when to go home |
| `supplies` or `tele` live, `gold` dead | supply use and restock timing, not money |
| `protect` live | cures carried and their order of use, and avoiding the casters that blind and confuse |
| `esp` live | detection: spells, rods and potions already carried, used on arrival |
| `know` live | the price of the honest view: identification order and learning lore sooner |
| `all` dead | the executor is the limit (movement, targeting, when to engage): phase 2 decides whether to port the Borg's danger sum and fight core |
| all single arms dead, `all` live | the gains only come together: phase 3's factorial is the right tool, with these axes as its factors |

## Phase 2: Borg reference

The 10-27 review asked for the APW Borg on the same engine as a headroom check; it was never run. The fork already
has a headless front end (`main-borg.c`, `angband -mborg -n`) and a JSON log (`borg-json-log.c`).

- **What it is.** A reference, not a rival. It reads the game's monster data directly and its auto-birth here knows
  every rune, so it isn't an honest-view player. The review also found that some of its early rules never fire on
  4.2 names.
- **Settings** (`borg-init.c` defaults, checked in the log): `borg_plays_risky`, `borg_cheat_death` and
  `borg_munchkin_start` false. Its birth is fixed by `borg_respawn_race` and `borg_respawn_class` (Human Warrior in
  this fork).
- **Classes** (user, 2026-11-09: the pure casters are included): Warrior, Mage, Priest and Druid, all Human, the
  Borg's class set through `borg_respawn_class` in `borg.txt` (an index in `class.txt`'s order; the log confirms the
  class born). The casters are where busho is weakest (fresh Mages: 10% reach DL10, 0.7% DL15).
- **Pilot.** 10 Borg lives a class, to check that the headless Borg plays each one (it casts, shops and descends,
  and every life ends in a death or the cap) and to measure its games per hour, which isn't known.
- **Run.** Per class, 200 Borg lives and 200 busho D25 lives (`--race Human --class <class>`; busho's casters with
  `spellfx,spellfight` as in the base). The common clock is game turns: the Borg is capped at the 95th percentile
  of busho's final game turn for that class from phase 0, and a second set of 50 Borg lives a class runs with a cap
  ten times that, to see what patience buys.
- **Logged per life.** Deepest level, highest CL, death cause, game turns, and on first arrival at each depth: CL,
  HP, AC, speed, and the counts of escapes and cures.
- **Reading rule.**
  Read per class, at DL15 for Warriors and at DL10 for the casters (too few busho casters reach DL15 for 200 lives
  to show a ratio).
  - Borg's share reaching that depth at the same cap is at least twice busho's: it is the template for that class.
    Compare its strength and kit on arrival at each depth with busho's, and its time per level. Its preparedness
    table and its danger sum over everything in view (the review's item e) become phase 3 factors. For a caster,
    also compare which spells it casts and how often: busho's spell rules are its newest and least tested.
  - Only the long cap is better: patience is the difference, which points at the descent margin and the score, not
    at tactics.
  - Neither: record it and drop the Borg as a template.

## Phase 3: factorial screens

Every screen so far tested one rule against the base, so each game informed one question. A factorial design draws
every factor at random in every game, so each game informs all of them.

### Design

- **Factors are binary**: a flag on or off, or a setting at its base value or one alternative.
- **Foldover pairs.** Each start is played twice with the same seed: once with a random draw of all factors, once
  with every factor flipped. Each pair is then a paired comparison for every factor at once, and main effects are
  clear of two-factor interactions. N pairs estimate each main effect about as well as a dedicated N-pair screen.
- **Settings.** busho reads `RULEPARAM=name=value,...` through one accessor, `param(name, base)`, at the constants
  listed below. With `RULEPARAM` unset the base replays identically (canary).
- **Flags that only work together** (packlight and floorpick; devicefx and its three dfx flags) are one factor.
- **Driver.** `driver19.py`, stage `factor`: starts as driver18 (bank) or driver17 (fresh), factor draws seeded
  from the start's seed, both games of a pair logged with their factor vector.

### Analysis

`hazard.py factor`: two log-linear rate models over all games, deaths per action and new depths per action, each
with stage and class terms and one coefficient per factor. A factor's two coefficients are its death and pace
ratios; applied to the base table (`scaled`) they give its PH. Standard errors by bootstrap over starts (lineages on
the bank). Tests: the simulator in `hazard.py` extended to several factors with known effects, and the type I error
measured.

### Controls: the method is checked before it is trusted

Round 1 includes three factors with known answers on the bank:

| Factor | Known result | Passes if |
|---|---|---|
| blindsafe (taken out of the base, as a factor) | PH 0.68 and 0.64 at two screens | estimate within 0.50–0.85 |
| deepgate | PH 1.43 at 150 pairs, pace ratio 0.82 | estimate above 1.15 |
| wor2 | PH 1.00 at 164 pairs | 80% interval includes 1 |

If a control fails, the factorial stops and screening goes back to driver18 until the cause is found.

### Round 1 factors

The final list is fixed at launch, after phases 1 and 2, with at most 16 factors. Candidates:

- **Carried over** (between the bounds or under-powered before): devicefx with the three dfx flags (PH 0.90), confesc
  (0.86, then 1.03 on the blindsafe base), teleshop, packlight, fearfix, curefx, gearset after a bug check.
- **Settings never varied** (`busho-player.ts`, the descent block near line 5960):
  - `clmargin`: descend only at CL ≥ DL + 4 from DL4, with a new `farm` behaviour for a spent level (take `<` then
    `>` for fresh levels instead of waiting); base is today's gate;
  - the kill quota doubled;
  - the too-long threshold halved;
  - `minHealingForDescent` doubled;
  - `minPhaseForEscape` doubled;
  - the restock target for oil doubled;
  - lowheal's threshold (35% against 50% HP).
- **Whatever phases 1 and 2 nominate.**

### Runs and decisions

- **Bank round**: 2,000 foldover pairs (4,000 games, about 3.5 hours), PH(20) from DL10. Expected sd of log PH per
  factor about 0.07, so effects near PH 0.89 become visible, against 0.82 for a 700-pair screen of one rule.
- **Fresh round**: 2,000 foldover pairs at 60,000 actions (about 6 hours), PH(15) from a fresh start. The descent
  settings are judged here, on whole lives: on deep starts the margin is already spent, and a 20,000-action cap
  rejects anything patient by construction. The sd is measured in round 1.
- **Nomination**: a factor with z ≥ 2.0 on its PH and no control failure. Class by factor effects are computed for
  nominated factors only.
- **Adoption**: the nominated factors as one bundle against the base on fresh seeds, by the existing sequential
  screen (looks at 150, 300 and 700 pairs). With 16 factors about one false nomination a round is expected, which is
  what the confirmation is for.
- **After round 1** the factorial is the standing overnight job. A new rule idea needs a flag, not its own screen.

### The games as model data

Every factorial game writes its factor vector, its danger log, and the decision records (state text, options, the
choice made) for the decisions M2 has passed. Because the settings are drawn at random, these games show what
happens under choices the base never makes, which neither the present logs nor one-override rollouts do. M3 trains
and scores on them.

## Phase M2: decision headroom

After phase 1. A decision goes to the model only if a perfect chooser would gain something there. This is the gate
the six questions should have had.

- **Candidates** come from phase 1's live axes:

  | Live axis | Decision | Options |
  |---|---|---|
  | strength (`cl10`, `hp2`) | at each level entry | descend; farm at this depth (fresh levels by `<` then `>`); go up one |
  | supplies, `gold` | in town, with the gold in hand | cures first; escapes first; gear first; oil and ammo first |
  | `protect`, `esp`, or the Borg's danger sum | at first sight of a monster not yet adjacent | engage; fight at range; move out of its view; leave by the stairs |
  | `offense` | on finding a weapon or armour piece | wear it; keep the worn one (the what-if stats are in the prompt) |

  Each option needs an executor the rules can run for some hundreds of actions. `farm` and "move out of its view"
  are new code.
- **Measure.** The long-horizon check: 400 decisions from D25 games, each option forced, 8 paired seeds, 2,000
  actions, the pick made on seeds 1–4 and scored on seeds 5–8.
- **Gate.** The held-out gain of the rollout pick is at least half of what M1's check set showed for the weakest of
  the three knocked-out rules. That number doesn't exist yet; it is written here when M1's step 1 finishes. If M1
  found the check too blunt to see a known gain, M2 waits for a better check.
- **Cost.** About 3 farm hours a decision.

## Phase M3: the ladder

For each decision that passes M2, every kind of decider is measured on the same states, so the model's value is a
difference between rungs, not a single score.

| Rung | Decider | What the step up to it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | the base rule | |
| 1 | the best fixed setting from phase 3 | what tuning gives with no state-dependence at all |
| 2 | a hand rule on a few features, written after reading the labels | what a person extracts from the same data |
| 3 | trees on the danger-log features | what learning gives without a decision model |
| 4 | Laya fine-tuned on the state text, at 1,000, 4,000 and all labels | the decision model with our data, and its data appetite |
| 5 | Jev 1.13 zero-shot (and with examples in the prompt, if the API allows) | the decision model with no data of ours |
| 6 | the rollout pick | the ceiling the labels allow |

- **Data.** Rollout labels as in M1, plus phase 3's games. To avoid session 13's trap (labels that value one
  override with the rules carrying on, which didn't add up in play), a factorial factor `explore:<decision>` makes
  the choice at random at one occurrence in five through a whole game and logs it. Outcomes from those games value
  a choice in the company of other departures from the rules.
- **Offline score.** On held-out decisions, the share of rung 6's gain over rung 0 that each rung captures.
- **Play.** Rungs 1 to 5 as arms against rung 0 by the sequential screen, then a confirmation of the best. Jev arms
  as in M1 (4 games at a time, 300 pairs, the ledger).
- **Reported per decision**: rung 4 − rung 3 (the model against a plain learner), rung 5 − rung 0 (the model without
  our data), rung 4 − rung 1 (the model against tuning), and each as a share of the ceiling. The row is added to
  "What is known so far about the model's value".

**What the project can then say**, whichever way the numbers fall: for tactical, knowledge and strategic decisions
separately, whether a decision model brings anything a hand rule or trees don't; whether it needs our labels or
comes with the knowledge; how many labels it needs; and how much of what a perfect chooser could gain it captures in

## Risks

- **Cheats change what the rules do**, not only how strong the character is (`cl10` and diving, `gold` only when the
  character goes home). Hence the depth-stratified death ratio as the reading, and both a deep and a fresh run.
- **Effects may not add.** The foldover design protects main effects from two-factor interactions, not from larger
  ones; the confirmation of the bundle is the safeguard.
- **Half the factorial's games play without blindsafe**, and many play worse than base, so its games aren't base
  games and don't join the pooled progress table.
- **The Borg may fail for reasons unrelated to play** (4.2 names, the headless front end). A life that ends without
  a death message or a cap is dropped and counted.
- **M1's question is wider than the rule it replaces**, so a model can fail by acting where the rule stays quiet.
  That is part of what is measured; the offline step shows it before any game is played.
- **Jev arms are slower** (about 200 ms a call). They are capped by actions and game turns, not wall time, and
  matched progress is reported.
- **`farm` and the `/cheat` hooks are new code** in the paths every game runs. Both are off unless asked for, and
  the canary replay covers the off state.

### Review notes (amendments to the plan above)

Checked against the code and the 10-31 data on 2026-11-09:

1. **The state text can't show the blind hole.** `buildStateText` has HP, a status line and `Heals:N` (every Cure and
   Healing potion pooled), but no hits taken, no HP trend and no messages. A model asked "blind: rest or cure?" can't
   see what blindsafe keys on (hits by something unseen). The blind question's situation lines add, from what the UI
   shows: hits taken and HP lost since going blind, turns blind, the last 3 messages, Cure potions by kind (CLW, CSW
   and CCW all cure blindness in 4.2), on stairs or not, and remembered monsters within 7. Without this, the
   zero-shot arms would fail for a reason that says nothing about the model.
2. **Labels need full play rules in rollouts.** Rollout mode switches off tryunknown, restock, worwait, gearid and
   invfix unless `ROLLOUT_FULL_RULES=1` (added 11-01). M1's labels use it. tryunknown's knockout can't be labelled
   without it at all: the rule itself is gated off in rollouts.
3. **The +0.085 in the first reading rule was measured with those rules off** (the 10-31 fight/tactic check predates
   the switch; the strategic check had it). To compare like with like, 60 of the 10-31 dangerous decisions are
   relabelled with full rules (about 40 farm minutes) before the reading rule is applied.
4. **Policy labels next to override labels.** blindsafe acts about 4 times a game (about 4 blind episodes a game in
   the knocked-out base, in 67% of games). An override label forces one option at one episode, and the knocked-out
   base rests at the next, which is session 13's trap. On the check set, each option is also labelled sticky (forced
   at every blind episode of the rollout, as `ROLLOUT_STAY_ACTIONS` does for descent). If the sticky pick shows the
   hole and the single one doesn't, the 10-31 check was blunt in exactly this way, and M2 measures with sticky labels.
5. **When the question is asked**, fixed now: at the first blind action with a monster adjacent, a hit taken, or a
   moving monster remembered within 7 in the last 100 game turns; then again after each new hit; at most 6 times an
   episode. Options: rest, cure (the weakest potion that cures blindness), stairs (only when on them), walk away (a
   step away from the remembered threat; new executor).
6. **Data budget.** About 4 qualifying episodes a game, so 4,400 decisions (at most 4 a game) need about 1,100
   knocked-out bank games: about 1.5 farm hours. Snapshots are kept for the blind question only (about 300 MB).

### M2: training labels for fight and recall (launched 2026-11-11 11:00)

The training labels come from a new collection: D25 on bank starts, seed 9800000, 900 games, types fight and recall.
`collect.py --exclude-clusters` leaves out every bank lineage behind a check game (367 lineages), so no training
game shares a character with the check sets. The pipeline:
1. m1_select takes up to 4 decisions a game and 2,400 a type;
2. `m1_labels.sh` makes sticky training labels (4 seeds, 500 actions);
3. Laya fine-tunes one question at a time on the GPU;
4. each model is scored on the six-question check sets, beside the constants, Jev and Laya zero-shot.

The question it answers: can labels give a model the state-dependent part that the pretrained models miss?

**Results (2026-11-11 15:30).** The collection had 897 games with fight records and 566 with recall records.
Sticky labels came back for 2,388 of 2,402 fight decisions and 2,014 of 2,068 recall decisions (2.0 h and 1.6 h).

**Reading:**
- **Recall is learnable, but not by this fine-tune.** The trees, on 20-odd numbers from the danger log, beat the

- **Fight's state signal is not learnable from these features.** The trees only match the best constant. Its

- **The information is in the model's input.** The state text gives HP, CL and DL, speed, nearby and recent monsters,

**What this says about the question so far.** At blind, the decision model's value was its prior: the constant rule
any expert knows. At recall, there is real state-dependent value. A small model on hand-picked features finds it
from about 2,000 labels. The text model, at this label budget and with this training recipe, doesn't. M3's ladder
now has a clear rung to test: a decider that reads the state can beat a rule. Whether Laya can be that decider
depends on its training, not on its input.

**Next:**
1. Fine-tune Laya on targets with less noise: the argmax of u_mean where the margin is clear, or more seeds per

2. A recall play arm with the trees (served through the decision policy like the model arms) against the base, to
   test the offline gain in play.
3. Fight: add monster-kind features (level, speed, breath, ranged) to the trees before concluding anything.

| 1,000 | +0.44 (0.13) | +0.17 (0.10) | 9.2% / 13.9% |
| 2,000 | +0.08 (0.14) | −0.04 (0.14) | 24.8% / 26.5% |
| to game end | −0.12 (0.24) | −0.21 (0.24) | 93.5% / 84.4% |

Blind on the same method (blindsafe against the knockout, 174 pairs): +0.44 at 500, +0.35 at 2,000, +0.60 at game
end. The Jev and Laya arms are alike.

**Findings:**
- **The rollout labels are faithful.** Play reproduces the offline gain at the label horizon, for recall and for
  blind.
- **Recall's gain is transient** (deaths postponed by about 1,500 actions; more deaths by game end). **Blind's
  persists.**
- **Most of recall's gain is the jump back to the deepest level.** In 54 of 294 pairs the character stood above its
  deepest level (by 2.7 levels on average), and those pairs score +2.15 at 500 actions. The other 240 score +0.06.
- **The training labels have the same defect.** 67 of 2,014 decisions (3.3%) are jump states, where recall −
  continue is +5.47. It is −0.04 on the rest, so the whole edge is the jump, and the best constant without it is
  continue. 98.5% of jump states have DL ≤ 12, which is the stump and what DL − CL keys on: the trees learned the
  artefact. (Jump states were identified from the gains, because records lack the deepest level.)
- **The pace loss is not town time** (20 of the first 500 actions against 5). The mechanism is unknown.
- **The gate held:** the trees beat always-recall in play (PH 1.11 against 1.27). What was wrong was the offline
  constant. Report trees and stump as "no gain shown" (PH 95% roughly 0.75–1.64), not as losses.

**Adopted:**
1. **Progress from the deepest level so far** (PH's clock): records log the character's max depth at the decision,
   and labels are re-scored with it.
2. **Persistence test:** the check set is labelled with checkpoints at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 actions. A question
   passes only if its gain over the best constant holds at 2,000.
3. **Aligned-play canary** in `m1_offline.py`, run on the archived deep screens with known verdicts. A candidate
   utility passes if, at its horizon, its sign matches the end-of-game difference on each screen.
4. **A terminal value only as a cost saver** after 1–3: P(death before the next stage advance), and actions to that
   advance. Not C(stage) per decision (its paired se is thousands of actions).
5. **Fight gets the same gate before any fine-tune,** because flee, like recall, defers exposure. Then: engage
   against flee, sticky scoped to the monster, share-target trees, and Laya with the working recipe. Add on-stairs
   to the fight text. Fight's share-tree +0.20 is unreplicated until then.

**The M2 gate now has four parts:** live in play; a noise-corrected ceiling; a state-dependent gain over the best
constant; and persistence to 2,000 actions. The recall result is a finding about labels: a short action horizon
rewards any option that defers risk.

### Persistence test (`runs/m1/2026-11-11-persist`)

The recall and fight check sets (the same 200 decisions each) were labelled to 2,010 actions, 4 seeds, with
checkpoints at 500, 1,000 and 2,000. All are scored from the deepest level (`m1_rescore.py --checkpoint`). Recall is
single override. Fight is single, and sticky scoped to the episode (`ROLLOUT_STICKY=episode`: the same target
monster while its asks keep coming).

| Labels, from the deepest level | @500 | @1,000 | @2,000 | Ceiling @2,000 (reliability) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recall, single: pick over the constant (continue) | +0.13 ± 0.04 | +0.22 ± 0.07 | **+0.27 ± 0.08** | +0.31 (0.36) |
| fight, single: pick over the constant | +0.06 ± 0.07 (flee) | +0.08 ± 0.07 (flee) | +0.01 ± 0.07 (throw) | +0.16 (0.18) |

Deaths by option at 2,000: recall 26.7% against continue 32.2%; fight, flee 54% against melee 56%.

**Readings:**
- **Recall passes,** and is now a different question. Measured from the deepest level, recall and continue are equal
  on average (mean difference −0.02 at 2,000), so no constant gains. The state-dependent part is real: the true-effect
  sd is 0.81 and the held-out pick gains +0.27, growing with the horizon rather than fading. The value is in *when* to
  recall. It was masked before by the jump credit (which made always-recall look best) and by the short horizon.
- **The M2 recall trees learned the jump, not this.** They were trained on 500-action labels from the current depth.
  Their failure in play doesn't speak to the question as now measured.
- **Fight doesn't clearly pass.** The single-override signal fades by 2,000. The episode-sticky signal grows to
  +0.12 but is 1.6 se, with reliability 0.30. Hold it, and don't fine-tune on it yet.
