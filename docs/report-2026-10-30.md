## Summary

*pending: three or four sentences once sessions 23 and 24 are in.*

- **Rules track.** Eleven rule changes since session 13, adopted in five rounds, each confirmed on new starts.
- **Model track.** The fine-tuned decision model (Laya, rollout labels) was never promoted over the rules. The
  learned danger model predicts deaths well (it passes its gate) but neither rule built on it helped play.
- **Measurement.** Verdicts moved from a capped per-game score to PH(10), a cap-free replacement-cost ratio, judged by
  a group-sequential screen.

## What was built

- **C engine bridge.** busho plays vanilla 4.2.6 (`angband-c`, branch `jev-http`) through `main-http.c`, an HTTP
  front end that sends only what the player's screen shows: the map as seen, monster recall as the UI shows it,
  items under their known names, the status bar and the level feeling.
- **Honest view.** `tools/honest-view.ts` turns that view into busho's state. Rules and prompts see nothing the UI
  doesn't (lore starts empty; "breeds explosively" only after it has been seen).
- **Unattended farm.** `tools/overnight/driver*.py` runs 13–16 games at a time with paired starts (each start played
  by every arm), a replay canary at the start of every session, disk guards and per-stage reports. Sessions 2–23 ran
  unattended, 5 to 40 hours each.
- **Kev/Laya decision model** in Docker (`compose.yaml`), with a System One client in busho for local Kev or hosted Jev.
- **Danger model.** Per-action danger logs, a LightGBM trainer, and an in-game evaluator that matches LightGBM to 1e-12.
- **Spells and items by effect.** The bridge sends each spell's engine effects, with the dice the spell browser shows,
  each known item kind's effects, and the timed effects the status bar shows. `tools/spell-roles.ts` maps effects to
  roles (heal, cure, nourish, escape, detection, buff, attack, control, dig), so no rule names a spell or class. The
  danger logger records resources and actions by role, and the decision prompts can list them (`DECISION_ROLES=1`).

## Measurement

A capped score (depth reached by the 20,000-action cap, minus a death penalty) judged sessions 2–16. About a third of
games are still alive at the cap, so anything that changes pace was judged at an arbitrary cutoff.

From session 17 the verdict score is **PH(10)** (`tools/overnight/hazard.py`):
- each game becomes segments by its deepest level so far (stage), ending in an advance, a death or the cap;
- per stage, deaths and advances per action give C(k), the expected actions for a fresh character to first reach
  DL k, counting deaths and restarts;
- a variant's stage-stratified death-rate and advance-rate ratios are applied to the base's table:
  PH(10) = C_var(10) / C_base(10). Below 1 is better.

A variant is screened against the base on paired starts, with looks at 150, 300 and 700 pairs: O'Brien–Fleming
efficacy bounds (z 3.60, 2.55, 1.67; one-sided α 0.05 overall), a futility stop, and no adoption while deaths at stage 7
and deeper are up with p < 0.05. An adopted set is then confirmed on new starts by the same screen. In simulation the
screen holds type I error at 0.03–0.04.

### What the rules learned about 4.2.6

For a player who last played in the 1990s, several of these are changes since then.

- **No pseudo-ID; identification is by runes (4.1 onward).** Items show `{??}` while they have unknown runes. Runes are
  learned by wielding, in combat, over time while worn, or when a property takes effect. Before gearid, busho scored
  equipment on known numbers only and never bought an upgrade (0 purchases in 873 town visits in session 20).
- **Selling pays nothing by default** (`birth_no_selling`: more gold drops instead). A sale still identifies the item.
- **Unknown potions are worth drinking.** tryunknown was the largest single gain (screen PH(10) 0.67). Characters then
  carry the potions they've learned, including Neutralize Poison, which busho never bought.
- **Poison** costs 1 HP a turn and stops regeneration. Cure Light and Cure Serious Wounds don't cure it; Cure Critical
  Wounds and Neutralize Poison do. Poison was the top killer in most sessions.
- **The Alchemist always stocks** Cure Light Wounds, Phase Door, Word of Recall and Remove Curse. Cure Serious and Critical
  Wounds are only sometimes in stock, and Teleportation scrolls are in no ordinary store.
- **Word of Recall read in town and then the stairs taken** lands on DL1, and recall then pulls the character up. busho
  did this in 269 of 329 town readings in session 22 (worwait, session 23).
- **Digging.** The tunnel command repeats up to 99 turns, so each futile try costs about 100 turns of food and light.
  Granite needs digging skill above 40. Futile tunnelling, not shopping, caused most starvation (digskill, session 23).
- **Monsters that never move**, and touch or gaze when next to the player: jellies, molds, mushroom patches, and the
  floating, bloodshot, disenchanter, radiation and evil eyes. A rot jelly's touch eats food (one Warrior lost 21
  rations in 75 actions and starved); a bloodshot eye's gaze blinds for 2d6.
- **Blindness.** Blind characters can't read but can quaff, walk and take stairs, and Cure Light, Serious and Critical
  Wounds all cure blindness.
- **Level feelings** come as two numbers; the danger number (LF:n) is shown on arrival. With connected stairs (on by
  default) a character arrives on a staircase leading back.
- **New classes.** The Blackguard (4.2) starts with 70 gold after its kit, against 330–410 for the other classes busho plays;
  its Whirlwind Attack wasn't worth casting (whirl, session 16).

## Model track

### A fine-tuned decision model (sessions 1–16)

busho asks six strategic questions (fight or flee; heal, flee or fight; recall; descend; explore or descend; swap
equipment). Zero-shot, no local model read the game state: on 16 clear-cut cases Kev-0.8B and Laya scored 38–50%, against
44% for a constant answer. So Laya was fine-tuned on rollout labels: snapshot the game at a decision, force each option,
let the rules play on for 50–150 actions with 8 seeds, and label by the outcome.

- **Labels were reproducible** (split-half agreement 80–84%) and the models beat the rules on held-out labels (about 60%
  against 40%).
- **Play didn't follow.** The best arm, trainH gated at confidence 0.1 with a cooldown (H-c10), won 51.2% of decided pairs
  over three sessions (p 0.077) and was never promoted.
- **Why (session 13's check):** each label values one override with the rules taking over afterwards. Overrides close
  together don't add up: about 95% of the gain predicted for the overrides the cooldown blocked was lost in play. H-c10's
  best case on paper was about +0.2 levels a game, under what one session's tournament can see.
- **Rule fixes moved the score more.** fire and lowheal together gained about 6 times H-c10's pooled edge. The planned
  16-hour model tournament was cancelled in favour of the rule screen.

### A learned danger estimate (sessions 17–20)

Labels are observed outcomes on every action of every logged game, so they are dense and need no rollouts. A LightGBM
model on 123 features from the player's view (including monster names, so it learns what a name means from the games).

| Model | Held-out | Hand estimate (busho's own) | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dmg_5` (HP lost in 5 turns) | MSE 0.00040, R² 0.42 | 0.00058, R² 0.15 | passes |
| `death_20` (death within 20 turns), 6,079 games | log loss 0.00645, AUC 0.926 | 0.00693, AUC 0.792 | passes |

Calibration: about 2–3 times too low in the middle (predicted 0.005 → observed 0.015) and too high at the top
(predicted 0.77 → observed 0.46), so thresholds were set on observed rates.

Both uses in play were rejected:
- **dangerml** (session 19): `meleeTooCostly` read predicted damage instead of the monster's blow averages. PH(10) 1.50.
  `dmg_5` predicts damage under the current play from this state; before contact that is near zero, so the rule fought
  far more. A state-level prediction can't stand in for "what will engaging this monster cost".
- **dangermlflee** (session 20): Borg-style flee and stairs triggers on `death_20`. PH(10) 1.05. The model saw 196 of 201
  deaths coming; four in five came after the Phase Door and Cure Light Wounds had run out, so fleeing earlier spent the
  same few scrolls sooner. That pointed the rules track at supplies, then identification.

### Spells, and capabilities lost to mismatches

busho was written against angband-ts, whose state is omniscient and whose spell names differ from 4.2.6's. Under the C
player view some rules never fire, or fire wrongly. An audit (every field busho reads against what the bridge sends,
every hardcoded name against 4.2.6's data, and the commands that passed no game time in session 23's confirm arm, 707
games) found:

- **Spells.** busho chose spells from name lists. Rangers never cast Remove Hunger (learned in 101 games; 12
  starvation deaths in that arm) or Herbal Curing (cures poison, cuts and stun). Blackguards cast nothing ("Berserker"
  in the list, "Berserk Strength" in 4.2.6). Paladins cast Bless 8,685 times with no check that it was already active.
- **Fear.** busho attacks by walking into a monster. Afraid, 4.2.6 answers "You are too afraid to attack" and no game
  time passes; busho tries again. 1,288 runs of 30 or more such commands in 221 games, 10 deaths while afraid. Fear
  cures (Boldness, Heroism, Berserk Strength, which every Warrior starts with) were picked up about 190 times and never
  drunk.
- **Summoners** never recognised (the bridge sends spell names as the engine's `S_KIN`; the check never matched).
- **Speed potions** drunk only at DL10 and deeper: picked up in 100 games, and 45 of those characters died with one unused.
- **Strong healing potions** looked for by "Cure" in the name, so Healing, \*Healing\* and Life were never drunk.
- **A full pack**: every book kept (426 pickups of other realms' books, 4 of the class's own) and every known potion,
  including Poison and Blindness. 25 Cure Critical Wounds and 18 Word of Recall purchases failed for want of a slot.

Session 25 measures the fixes in three arms:
- **spellfx**: spells chosen by role. Nourish before food, cures before potions, buffs only when not active and above
  half HP, monster detection on arrival and every 300 actions, stair detection when ready to descend, dig spells,
  mana kept for an escape spell.
- **spellfight** (with spellfx): attack spells at a target square when they beat melee; sweep attacks when outnumbered;
  Leap into Battle; scare-all and control bolts before fleeing.
- **auditfix**: the fear, summoner, speed potion, healing potion and pack fixes together.

Session 25 launched at 07:45 on D24 (seed 4900000, all classes), after its canary replayed session 24's confirmation.

## Known problems and next steps

- **The default `danger` rule reads game data.** `meleeTooCostly` takes each monster's blow averages from
  `monster.json` by name, which the player hasn't necessarily seen. A lore-based estimate (`seenMeleePerTurn`) exists.
  Switching removes information the current results were earned with, so it needs its own screen.
- **Devices later in the game.** devices and devices2 were rejected on PH(10), which the farm's DL1–9 games dominate;
  at DL10+ there are too few deaths to tell. Next: devices chosen by effect (Teleport Other, Stone to Mud, slow and
  hold wands, Mapping, Rod of Recall, which the devices rules never use), tested on a new bank of DL10–20 starts with
  a deeper score (plan doc: "Devices by effect, tested on deep starts").
- **Not used at all:** recharging (Scroll of Recharging at the Alchemist; Recharging for Mages and Rogues), and the
  Staff of Teleportation (Magic shop, 2,000 gold). Teleportation scrolls are only in the Black Market in 4.2.6, and
  busho's shop lists look elsewhere first.
- Corridors that pass beside a stationary monster: stationmem avoids them; its deep deaths were up at look 2 and
  not significantly at look 3.
- Starvation that isn't tunnelling (D23 halved it in the confirmation; Remove Hunger is in spellfx).
- Early orc groups and uniques (Grip, Fang, Bullroarer, Grishnákh, Mughash).
- The danger model: use it for decisions it answers directly (am I in danger now), and retrain on the adopted rules'
  games with the new role fields.
- Minor: slays and brands are ignored in busho's damage estimate; the decision prompt's "Abilities" line is always empty.
